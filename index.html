      Planting checklist

      • Pit 60 × 60 × 60 cm, with the topsoil kept aside
      • About 3 m from the next sapling, clear of walls, pipes and wires
      • Topsoil mixed with compost or FYM and a little neem cake
      • Root ball intact, collar at ground level, soil firmed
      • Bamboo stake with soft ties, and a tree guard
      • Saucer basin about 1 m across, 5–8 cm of dry-leaf mulch
      • Watered right after planting

      How to plant a sapling

      At the start of the monsoon
      1. Mark the spot

        About 3 m apart, away from walls, pipes and overhead wires.

      2. Dig the pit

        60 cm × 60 cm × 60 cm. Keep the topsoil separate.

      3. Fill the pit

        Topsoil mixed with farmyard manure (FYM) or compost and a little neem cake against termites.

      4. Plant

        Remove the polybag without breaking the root ball. Set the sapling upright at collar level.

      5. Firm the soil

        Press the soil around the root ball to remove air pockets.

      6. Stake and guard

        A bamboo stake with soft ties, and a tree guard against grazing.

      7. Basin and mulch

        A saucer basin about 1 m across with a raised rim, covered with 5–8 cm of dry leaves.

      8. Water

        Water right away, then regularly. Saplings need care for about three years.

      How the tracker works

      • Every sapling gets an ID in order: T-001, T-002 and so on. Write it on the label tied to the tree guard.
      • A sapling needs water when nobody has watered it for 3 days. Planting counts as its first watering.
      • Its health comes from the latest visit: Healthy, Stressed or Dead.
      • Survival rate (%) = saplings alive ÷ saplings planted × 100. The Forest Department counts 60% or more as a success.
      • Each QR label holds the tracker’s link with the sapling’s ID at the end, like #T-003. Scanning it opens that sapling.

      Protect the soil around it

      • Mulching

        Dry leaves and straw absorb raindrop impact, keep the soil moist and add organic matter.

      • Half-moon bunds

        Crescent-shaped bunds on the downhill side trap runoff and silt on slopes.

      • Vegetative strips

        Grass and vetiver slips across slopes and drain edges slow runoff and hold the soil.

      • Composting

        Compost fallen leaves instead of burning them, and return them to the saplings.

      • Protection

        Guards, stones or a living fence of thorny Karvand stop trampling and vehicle damage.

      Project phases

      This tracker covers phase 6
      1. Area survey

        Meet residents; note bare patches, erosion and planting spots.

      2. Planning

        Fix sites, species, tools, compost, water and permissions.

      3. Awareness

        Talks, posters and door-to-door visits; families adopt a sapling.

      4. Plantation drive

        Plant, stake, guard and water each sapling.

      5. Soil protection

        Mulch, saucer basins, grass strips and composting.

      6. Monitoring

        Log watering and health; follow the survival rate.
